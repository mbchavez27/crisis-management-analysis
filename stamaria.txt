Maria (2010) grouped Filipino negative social emotions into threeclusters that differ in intensity and in five components: focal event,appraisal (blame and control), action tendency, regulation, and level ofrelationship invoked.In the descriptions below, "the actor" is the person feeling theemotion (here, the commenter) and "the other" is the person or thingthe emotion is directed at.Cluster 1: Suklam, Muhi, Poot (highest intensity)  Focal event: the other violates a moral order or behaves unethically.  Blame: the other is to blame.  Control: the actor can control the emotion by choosing to ignore the    incident.  Action tendency: if a relationship with the other exists, it is    reduced to mere civility.  Regulation: the actor controls the emotion, but if it is too intense,    distress is expressed, commonly through crying.  Relationship invoked: kapwa (shared moral humanity).Cluster 2: Sama ng loob, Galit, Tampo (moderate intensity)  Focal event: the other fails to meet expectations or obligations.  Blame: the actor is to blame for feeling the emotion.  Control: the actor can control the emotion by reframing and suspending    judgment of the other.  Action tendency: the relationship does not change if it is close; it    turns from warm to cold if it is not close.  Regulation: the actor controls the expression of the emotion.  Relationship invoked: hindi ibang tao (in-group, "one of us").Cluster 3: Buwisit, Asar, Ngitngit, Yamot, Pikon (lowest intensity)  Focal event: an event or person hinders the actor's immediate goals,    and the actor cannot "go with the flow" of the pressure.  Blame: the actor is to blame for not being tolerant enough.  Control: the actor can control the emotion by ignoring the irritation.  Action tendency: a temporary change in the relationship, from warm to    cold.  Regulation: the actor controls the expression of the emotion but may    show it nonverbally.  Relationship invoked: ibang tao (out-group, "outsider").Key point from the source: the clusters are defined by the pattern ofcomponents, not by the emotion word. Cluster 1 emotions arise even whenthe person has no direct relationship with the wrongdoer (e.g., watchingTV news of a crime): the moral violation itself creates the kapwa bond.Note that "galit" is placed in Cluster 2 in this framework, even thougheveryday use of "galit" often signals moral outrage.STEP 1 — SCREENNEG_SOCIAL_EMOTION  1 = the comment expresses a negative emotion (anger, resentment,      disgust, hurt, disappointment, irritation, annoyance) directed at a      person, group, organization, object, or situation.  0 = no such emotion (neutral information, questions, prayers,      condolences, pure sympathy, praise, jokes with no negative target).  If 0: set every remaining field to "NA" (lists to ["NA"]) and stop.STEP 2 — CODE THE COMPONENTSEMOTION_WORDS (list)  Record any of these 15 Filipino terms that appear literally, in any  inflected or affixed form (e.g., nakakabuwisit ?
